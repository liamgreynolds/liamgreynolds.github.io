Buzz Networks

I made this poster for a climate and development class at Middlebury. It’s called Buzz Networks — a play on the buzz that coffee gives you — and it maps the network of Uganda’s smallholder coffee industry as farms adapt to the impacts of climate change and a changing economy. Coffee is a lifeline for over 1.8 million households in Uganda, and up to 95% of the country’s coffee is grown by smallholder farms — Uganda is now Africa’s largest coffee exporter — but those same small farms are some of the most vulnerable to a changing climate, and they typically lack the capital to adapt on their own.

The poster mostly follows the coffee itself. A flow diagram at the top traces how coffee moves from smallholder farms through cooperatives, middlemen, and processors to international roasters, and the world map below sorts Uganda’s connections into three kinds: economic connections, climate adaptation connections, and climate stressor connections. In the top corner I follow one farm in particular — Jackson and Sylvia Mvyambi, who have farmed 1.5 acres in Uganda’s Western Region since 1996 — and you can trace their coffee along the purple line as they take up agroforestry practices like planting shade trees, mulching, and keeping bees. The poster is an attempt to create a partial map of actor-network-theory-inspired connections, to get a better idea of how climate and coffee interact in Uganda’s smallholder sphere.

My favorite part is the pair of maps showing where Arabica beans can grow now versus where they’ll still be able to grow in 2060. Arabica only grows at high elevations, and as the climate warms that band is shrinking uphill — but agroforestry can cool a farm by up to 2°C, which might be what saves it.
